Other ways in

The charts and exports are this website, over the Stick's Wi-Fi. A tethered computer can also type commands on the USB serial port at 115200 baud. Bluetooth advertises as Tremble and uses the Nordic UART service. The commands are the same: status, start, stop, rate, range, seconds, gyro on, wifi.

A CSV from another logger can be dropped on Analyze. Use columns named x, y, and z, in g, and a sample_rate_hz comment if there is no time column.

Peaks

    On the Stick

      How to take a reading

      1. Clamp or stick the magnetic back to a rigid part of the structure. A hand-held reading is the motion of your hand.
      2. Wait a few seconds so the mount stops ringing.
      3. Press Start on this page. Do not touch the Stick, and do not press its buttons, until the capture finishes.
      4. Open Analyze. Export a PNG for a report, or CSV if you want the samples in another program.

      What the numbers mean

      • RMS is the steady vibration level. Gravity and the resting tilt are removed before it is calculated.
      • Peak and crest factor (peak ÷ RMS) rise when the motion is impulsive rather than sinusoidal. A sine wave has a crest factor near 1.41.
      • Velocity is acceleration divided by frequency. Metric units are mm/s. US units are in/s. Machinery standards such as ISO 20816 use velocity RMS, and the limit depends on the machine. Compare the same point over time.
      • The spectrum uses a Hann window. A tone shows up as a peak at its frequency. The live spectrum is amplitude, so the axis starts at zero. Log amplitude, on Analyze, is dB relative to the peak, and those numbers sit at or below zero. Log frequency spreads the low structural modes apart.
      • Click a curve, or its name, to select it. Click the name again to clear it. Click the plot to add a point on that curve. Drag the point along the curve, or right-click it to remove it.
      • The spectrogram shows how those tones fade or shift after a tap. Color is strength. Time runs left to right.
      • EMA weight runs from off to 0.9. Higher follows the samples more closely, so a lower weight is the smoother line. The noise gate draws quiet samples as zero, on the live trace and on a saved capture. The spectrogram keeps the samples before the gate, so a gate does not cut stripes out of it. Raw samples can sit under the filtered line.
      • Live feed: Pause freezes the charts on the last samples. Chart update controls how often the page asks the Stick for new samples. Steady is the default. Slow is easier on the hotspot when the page keeps dropping. The trace crosses zero because gravity is already removed, so a still Stick wanders a little above and below. While it is running, the time axis slides forward with the clock. Spectrum peaks marks each frequency the live spectrum finds.
      • Signal switches both charts to acceleration, velocity, or displacement, and the scale fits that one. They are not drawn on top of each other, because a few g and a few mm/s are not the same size. Click a curve name and the scale follows that axis. Metric units are mm/s and µm. US units are in/s and inches.
      • Zoom: Time window and the Hz boxes crop the live charts. On Analyze, Time from, Time to, Hz from, and Hz to do the same for a saved capture.
      • Decay envelope draws the curve a clean ring would follow. The fit uses the raw samples, before the EMA smoother. Smoothing first flattens the hit and makes the damping look too high. If the trace pokes through the lines, the motion is not a clean log decay. A rattle does that.
      • Q and ζ come from the half-power width of the live acceleration peak, again on the raw samples. A high Q is a long ring. The bar turns amber when the ring is light, and blue when it dies quickly.
      • Noise floor: set the Stick on a rigid surface, like the floor, and measure. That RMS is how quiet this sensor is. Use it as the noise gate and the live trace lays that fuzz on zero.
      • RSS is the size of the vibration on all three axes together, so the mounting direction matters less.
      • Damping is estimated from the half-power width of the strongest peak. Set a baseline before a change, then compare the next capture.
      • The timeline plays the capture back. Pick a short window to inspect one tap.
      • Axis remap relabels the sensor axes when the Stick is mounted on its side.
      • Displacement integrates a second time. Treat it as an estimate. Gyroscope values are degrees per second at ±2000.

      Limits of this sensor

      The StickS3 uses a Bosch BMI270. It is a useful screening instrument for students and for field checks. It is not a calibrated laboratory accelerometer. Keep spectral conclusions below about 45% of the sample rate; the sensor's digital filter rolls off before the Nyquist frequency. Very small structural motion can sit near the sensor noise. The highest rate is 1600 Hz, so this cannot see ultrasonic bearing frequencies.

      Suggested starts

      • Structure: 400 Hz, ±2 g, 30 s, high-pass 0.3 Hz.
      • Machine: 1600 Hz, ±8 g, 10 s, high-pass 2 Hz, low-pass 500 Hz.
      • Impact: 1600 Hz, ±16 g, a few seconds, high-pass 1 Hz.

      Saved files use the .vibe format. CSV, JSON, and PNG are created in the browser from that capture.
